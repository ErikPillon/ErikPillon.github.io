A running list of short papers and essays that changed how a whole field thinks — the kind of thing you can read in an evening and then argue about for a decade. These are the ones I have actually read; the list grows slowly and on purpose.

### Warren Weaver — *Science and Complexity* (1948)

**Complex systems, biology.** Explores the role of complexity and self-organization across scientific disciplines, with an emphasis on biology. It quietly sketches chaos theory and information theory years before either had a name. → [PDF](/articles/warren_weaver_1948.pdf) · [my notes](/writing/science-and-complexity/)

### Eugene Wigner — *The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Mathematics in the Natural Sciences* (1960)

**Mathematics, physics.** On the uncanny fact that mathematics invented for its own sake keeps turning out to describe the physical world. → [PDF](/articles/eugene_wigner_1960.pdf)

### Abraham Maslow — *A Theory of Human Motivation* (1943)

**Psychology.** Proposes the hierarchy of needs, outlining human motivation and self-actualization.

### Peter Drucker — *The Age of Social Transformation* (1994)

**Sociology, management.** Discusses the profound social changes brought about by the rise of the knowledge society. → [PDF](/articles/peter_drucker_1994.pdf)

### Nicholas Carr — *Is Google Making Us Stupid?* (2008)

**Technology, psychology.** Examines the impact of internet usage, and search engines in particular, on cognition and human thinking. → [PDF](/articles/nicholas_carr_2008.pdf)
