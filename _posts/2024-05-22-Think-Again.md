The book is divided in 3 parts, that abstract the 3 level where rethinking acts: personal level, interpersonal level and sociological level.

The book builds from the fundamantal principle that being human, not only we fall trap constantly of several biases, but most importantly we even experience the "I'm not biased" bias. It is therefore important to not only be aware of the fact that what we consider as universally true might actually be wrong, but also that what used to be true in the past might not be true in the future. 

Knowledge is a continous process and as such, need to be constantly built, challenged, destroyed and rebuilt. 

### arrogance is ignorance plus conviction
Chapter 2 demistifies the effect of self confidence on our behaviour. If on one side we have the "armchair quarterback syndrome" (i.e., being too arrogant and self confident), on the other we have the impostor syndrome, leading to chronic immmobilism and insecure behaviour. Finding the right balance in the middl, what the author calls the "confident humility zone", is our goal. 
In case of doubt, we might be induced to adopt a more self confident approach to problems and tasks in life (after all, all the successfull people that we see every day are really self confident people) but the author argues that there's plenty of evidence in the academic literature that confidence is often the results of achieving results, not the cause of it. 

> Arrogance leaves us blind to our weaknesses. Humility is a reflective lens: it helps us see them clearly. Confident humility is a corrective lens, it enables us to overcome those weaknesses.

Chapter 3 helps us understand how we can take a proactive approach to rethinking and challenging our positions. Discovering ourself wrong should not be a painful moment, but instead a joyful and positive one: we just learnt something afterall! Overcoming the sense of ignorance and conviction by means of the pleasure that we get by our recent discovery should be the driving factor in challengin the small "dictator" that we have in our head that keeps us anchored to our old position (see also "Think fast and slow" by Daniel Kahneman in these regards). Chapter 4 follows immediately on this topic and shows us how to overcome a conflicting position not only with ourselves but also with other people, thus opening to the topic of conflict resolution and collective thinking. As Hegel said, the synthesys process of an idea need to come from the solved conflict from an idea and its anthysis, thus inducing a new better idea, bringing the best of both worlds.

Chapter 5 is about debating and convincing your audience or your interlocutor. Convincing someone is not a matter of being better documented (falling trap of the argument dilution) or having a stronger scientific evidence (and thus alienating your audience), but to have the best argument and the best attitude. When documenting yourself and trying to make an opinion, don't forget to also train yourself in conveying that idea and that opinion.

Second section ends with a discussion about prejudices and stereotypes and on the role of mediators in debates. Motivational interviewing is also discussed and is shown how an active listener can help decision makers to get to a "yes".

Section number 3 is a beatiful investigation in accepting unconvenient truths, accepting the never ending cyclic nature of the (re-)learning process and the overcoming the "we always did this way" attitude. There's no such a dangerous thing in associations and companies as "we always did this way". Being open to improvements, adjustments and changes is the key to success in the long term. The author argues that a fundamental step in this direction must be taken by teachers when already from the early steps of the educations system, students should be not taught to learn concepts, but rather to rethink concepts and to appreciate the rediscover an old concept in a new way or in a new perspective. 

## Summary
All in all this is a wonderful book that made me think a lot, bot on how I make my own opinions and on how I change them (either individually and as part of a society).
I was not particularly familiar with Adam Grant, the author of this book, but after checking him out on the web I discovered tons of amazing material that investigates different and collateral topics of this manuscript. I will definitely check out other works by him in the future.
